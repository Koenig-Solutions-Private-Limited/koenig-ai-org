# Performance Marketing Budget Allocation & Bid Strategy at Scale

## Why this course

Most performance marketers learn on one channel. They know how to set up a Google Search campaign, or how to run a Meta prospecting funnel. What they don't get exposure to is the cross-channel allocation question: when you have ₹1 crore to spend next month across Google, Meta, and Bing, how do you decide where each rupee goes?

This is the question that separates a campaign manager from a Performance Marketing Specialist. It requires a different mental model — one built on marginal returns, not individual platform metrics. It requires operational discipline: pacing cadences that don't blow the budget in week one. It requires bid strategy literacy: knowing when to let Google's algorithm drive, and when to stay manual. And it requires attribution honesty: understanding that Google and Meta are both counting the same conversion, and knowing how to de-duplicate.

This course covers all four. By the end, you'll have a repeatable framework for managing multi-channel budgets and a capstone brief that proves you can apply it.

## Course outline

### Chapter 1: The Cross-Channel Budget Mental Model — Marginal ROAS and Diminishing Returns

- **Duration**: 45 min
- **Prerequisites**: Course intro only
- **Learning objectives**:
  1. Explain the core difference between single-channel optimisation and cross-channel portfolio thinking
  2. Define marginal ROAS and use it to decide whether to increase or decrease spend on a channel
  3. Draw a diminishing returns curve from first principles and identify the "efficient frontier" spend zone
  4. Map a multi-channel budget hierarchy: account budget vs. campaign budget vs. ad-set/ad-group controls
  5. State why eCPA and ROAS benchmarks mislead without incrementality context
- **Key concepts**: Marginal ROAS, diminishing returns, efficient frontier, budget hierarchy (account/campaign/ad-set), portfolio thinking, attribution contamination
- **Hands-on exercise**: Given a fictional ₹50L/month account with performance data across Google Search, Meta Prospecting, and Meta Retargeting, apply the marginal ROAS rule to produce an optimised channel split. Justify each shift in one sentence.
- **Contrarian angle**: Most B2C marketers maximise channel ROAS. That's wrong — you should be maximising *marginal* ROAS across the portfolio. A channel with a 5x ROAS that's fully saturated is worth less than a channel at 3x ROAS that still has room to scale.

### Chapter 2: Budget Pacing, Reallocation Cadences, and Mid-Flight Decisions

- **Duration**: 50 min
- **Prerequisites**: Chapter 1
- **Learning objectives**:
  1. Calculate a daily spend target from a monthly budget and identify pacing danger signs early in the month
  2. Explain how Google's 2x daily budget allowance and Meta's delivery system can cause end-of-month overspend
  3. Design a weekly budget review checklist: what metrics to pull, what thresholds trigger reallocation
  4. Reallocate budget mid-flight without resetting campaign learning phases on Google Smart Bidding or Meta's delivery algorithm
  5. Use campaign end dates and shared budgets as pacing control levers on Google Ads
- **Key concepts**: Daily pacing formula, Google 2x daily allowance, Meta delivery system and spend curve, learning phase protection, shared budgets, campaign flight scheduling, weekly/monthly review cadence
- **Hands-on exercise**: Build a simple pacing tracker spreadsheet: columns for channel, monthly budget, days elapsed, spend-to-date, expected spend, variance, and alert flag. Populate it with sample data and flag which campaigns need budget adjustments this week.
- **Contrarian angle**: Most marketers set monthly budgets and check in at the end of the month. That's too late. A 3-day early-month overspend can blow 40% of the budget before you catch it. Daily pacing checks for the first 5 business days of the month prevent 90% of overspend incidents.

### Chapter 3: Automated Bidding Strategies — tCPA, tROAS, Max Conversions, and When to Go Manual

- **Duration**: 55 min
- **Prerequisites**: Chapter 2 (or strong existing Google Ads/Meta background)
- **Learning objectives**:
  1. Draw the automated bidding decision tree: campaign type → conversion volume → target availability → strategy
  2. Explain tCPA mechanics: what the algorithm optimises, how to set a realistic target, and the volume vs. efficiency trade-off
  3. Explain tROAS mechanics: revenue optimisation mode, when it requires transaction-level value signals
  4. Identify the 3 scenarios where Max Conversions (uncapped) is the right choice and the 2 scenarios where it destroys margin
  5. State the data volume minimums for Smart Bidding on Google (≥30 conversions/month recommended) and Meta's learning phase (≥50 conversions/7 days per ad set)
  6. Name two campaigns types where manual CPC or manual CPM is still the better call in 2026
- **Key concepts**: tCPA, tROAS, Max Conversions, Max Conversion Value, Smart Bidding learning phase, portfolio bid strategies, Advantage+ campaign budget (Meta), manual CPC, Enhanced CPC (deprecation), bid caps vs. cost caps vs. target costs (Meta), minimum conversion data thresholds
- **Hands-on exercise**: Given a sample campaign structure (branded search, non-brand search, Shopping, YouTube prospecting, Meta prospecting, Meta retargeting), assign the correct bid strategy for each campaign with a one-sentence rationale. Identify which campaigns do not yet have enough conversion data for Smart Bidding and what the fallback is.
- **Contrarian angle**: Smart Bidding is not a silver bullet. For new campaigns, low-volume campaigns (<30 conversions/month), and high-seasonality windows (sale events), algorithmic bidding frequently underperforms. The skill is knowing when to hand control to the machine and when to take it back.

### Chapter 4: Cross-Channel Attribution, ROI Forecasting, and Making the Budget Case

- **Duration**: 60 min
- **Prerequisites**: Chapters 1–3
- **Learning objectives**:
  1. Explain the four main attribution models (last-click, first-click, linear, data-driven) and what each over- or under-credits
  2. Describe the "walled garden problem": why Google, Meta, and Bing each claim credit for the same conversion and how to quantify the overlap
  3. Build a de-duplicated cross-channel attribution view using a simple dedupe heuristic (ordered-channel priority or time-decay)
  4. Construct a 30-day ROI forecast: input assumptions, sensitivity table, and worst/base/best scenario
  5. Design a geo holdout incrementality test: how to set it up, how to interpret the lift ratio, and how to use the result to justify a budget change to a CMO
- **Key concepts**: Attribution models, last-click vs. data-driven, walled garden double-counting, view-through vs. click-through attribution, geo holdout test, conversion lift study (Meta), incrementality, ROI forecast model, sensitivity analysis, stakeholder communication
- **Hands-on exercise**: Using a provided sample dataset with 3 channels' reported conversions, build a deduplicated attribution model in a spreadsheet. Then write a one-page "budget recommendation memo" addressed to a CMO that proposes shifting ₹10L/month from underperforming to outperforming channels, citing marginal ROAS and the incrementality result.
- **Contrarian angle**: "Attribution is solved by data-driven models" is a vendor fiction. Every DDA model is trained on platform-observed data — meaning Google's DDA optimises for Google and Meta's Advantage+ optimises for Meta. The only ground truth is a properly designed incrementality experiment. Everything else is directional.

## Capstone project

**Build a 90-day budget allocation brief for a ₹1Cr/month B2C performance marketing account.**

### Context
You are the incoming Performance Marketing Specialist at a travel-tech company (think: OTA, flight booking, or hotel platform). The company spends ₹1Cr/month across Google Search (branded + non-branded), Google Shopping, Meta Prospecting, Meta Retargeting, and Bing Search. The previous manager left no documentation. You have 30 days of historical data.

### Deliverable
A structured Google Doc (or equivalent) containing:
1. **Channel allocation table**: Proposed monthly budget per channel with marginal ROAS rationale for each decision
2. **Bid strategy matrix**: Recommended strategy per campaign type with data-availability check and fallback
3. **Pacing plan**: Daily spend targets for Week 1, weekly review triggers, and reallocation thresholds
4. **Attribution assessment**: Which model you will use and why; one-paragraph explanation of known double-counting risk
5. **30-day ROI forecast**: Table with input assumptions, expected spend, expected revenue, and a best/base/worst sensitivity range
6. **90-day roadmap**: Three initiatives (e.g., incrementality test, audience expansion, budget unlock) with sequencing rationale

### Verification criteria
- Channel allocation uses marginal ROAS logic (not simply historical allocation)
- Bid strategies match campaign type and data volume checks are present
- Pacing plan includes an explicit "early-month overspend trigger" rule
- Attribution section acknowledges the walled garden problem
- ROI forecast includes at least 3 input sensitivity levers
- 90-day roadmap includes one incrementality experiment

### Estimated time: 45 min (for learners who completed all 4 chapters)
