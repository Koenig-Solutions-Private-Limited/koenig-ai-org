Chapter 5 is game day. The previous four chapters each built one component skill — scoring ICP fit, reading buying signals, crafting signal-triggered sequences, and sourcing referral pipeline. This chapter puts all four to work simultaneously in a single, documented account pursuit, running from the moment a trigger fires to the moment a discovery meeting lands in your calendar.

## Before You Pursue: The Three-Input Account Selection Gate

Active pursuit is expensive. A two-week sprint on the wrong account costs exactly the same time as a sprint on the right one — so the entry gate is non-negotiable.

Before moving any net-new account into active pursuit, document three inputs:

1. **ICP fit score ≥70/100.** Below 70, the account enters nurture sequencing only — it does not get live pursuit. The [Growleads ICP scoring framework](https://growleads.io/blog/how-to-identify-and-target-your-ideal-b2b-customer-profile-icp/) builds the composite score across firmographic (35%), technographic (25%), trigger/intent (25%), and behavioral (15%) criteria. How to score and weight each category is covered in ch1.

2. **A confirmed trigger event.** A date-stamped, observable account-level change — a headcount surge, an executive hire, a funding announcement — that creates a "why now" for your outreach. Without a trigger, you are sending cold outreach to a dormant account; the prospect has no context for why they are hearing from you today rather than six months ago. Signal types and monitoring setup belong to ch2.

3. **A named two-persona buying committee with verified contacts.** Minimum: a Champion — typically the L&D Manager, who controls vendor evaluation — and an Economic Buyer — typically the IT Director or CFO, who controls budget release. Both personas need verified email or LinkedIn contact details before outreach begins. Reaching only the champion and ignoring the economic buyer means your deal stalls at procurement every time.

**Worked example:** InfoTech Solutions India — Mumbai-based IT services firm, 4,200 employees, Azure migration underway.

- ICP fit score: **94/100** — IT sector, Azure stack, no incumbent training vendor, FY27 headcount growth plan confirmed
- Trigger event: **28 Azure/Cloud Engineer job postings detected June 1–3, 2026** (a 40% month-over-month surge on LinkedIn and Naukri.com); outreach window June 5–10
- Buying committee: Gaurav Mehta, IT Training Manager (champion) + Arvind Sharma, VP IT Infrastructure (economic buyer) — both verified on LinkedIn

Three inputs documented. Account cleared for active pursuit.

## The 2-Week Pursuit Sprint

A minimum viable pursuit is **5 touches across email and LinkedIn over 2 weeks**, with every touch logged in your CRM: date, channel, contact name, content summary, prospect response, and next action. This is not optional record-keeping — without logged responses, you cannot calculate reply rate, which means you cannot evaluate what worked.

The InfoTech Solutions sprint, logged touch by touch:

| Touch | Date | Channel | Contact | Content | Response |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | June 5 | Email | Gaurav Mehta | Signal-triggered opener: referenced 28 Azure postings, AZ-104 July 15 batch seats, <75 words | No reply |
| 2 | June 7 | LinkedIn | Gaurav Mehta | Connection request: "Noticed InfoTech's Azure buildout — happy to share a case study?" | Accepted |
| 3 | June 7 | LinkedIn DM | Gaurav Mehta | Value-add: Koenig 200-person Azure certification case study link | Read, no reply |
| 4 | June 12 | Email | Gaurav Mehta | Problem-insight hook: "3 reasons Azure engineers fail AZ-104 first attempt" + Koenig pass rate vs. industry avg | **Positive reply: "Can we get on a call?"** |
| 5 | June 14 | Email | Arvind Sharma | ROI frame: ₹18L to certify 15 existing engineers vs. ₹72L to hire 3 pre-certified externally | Pending |

CRM log status: 5 touches, 2 channels, 1 positive reply, 1 meeting scheduled June 20.

<Callout type="warning">
**48% of reps never send a second email**, walking away from nearly half of all available replies. [LeadHaste's 2026 benchmark study](https://leadhaste.com/blog/outbound-sales-benchmarks-2026) puts it precisely: 58% of campaign replies come from the first email, but 42% come from follow-up touches. A 5-touch minimum is not an ambitious goal — it is the floor required to capture the full reply curve.
</Callout>

## Evaluating Your Sequence Performance

After the sprint closes, compare your results against published baselines before writing your pipeline report. The primary outcome metric is **positive reply rate** — replies indicating interest, curiosity, or a meeting request, excluding auto-replies and opt-outs.

Key benchmarks for IT training outbound ([LeadHaste, 2026](https://leadhaste.com/blog/outbound-sales-benchmarks-2026); [Growleads, 2026](https://growleads.io/blog/signal-based-outbound/)):

- Industry average cold email reply rate: **3.43%**
- IT/SaaS segment cold email range: **1.9–3.5%**
- Signal-enriched outbound target: **4–10%**
- Full signal-stacked sequences (two concurrent triggers): **8–10%**

The InfoTech sprint returned a 20% touch-level positive reply rate — a **5.8× outperformance** of the cold email baseline. That confirms signal-triggered, multi-channel outreach working as designed.

Now identify **one specific improvement**, grounded in touch-level data. In this sprint, Touches 1–3 generated zero replies; Touch 4 — the value-add email with a problem-insight hook — generated the positive reply. The data-supported recommendation: move the problem-insight email to Touch 2, immediately after LinkedIn connection acceptance, rather than to Touch 4. Evidence: sequences leading with a problem-insight hook rather than a product-offer CTA average 18% vs. 9% reply rates (Infraforge data, via LeadHaste). That is the specific A/B test to run in the next sprint.

## The One-Page Pipeline Report

Your manager's weekly pipeline review needs exactly one page: activity mapped to outcome, with a concrete next action for every account. Four columns — no substitutions, no omissions:

| Account | Touches Sent | Meetings Booked / Pending | Next Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| InfoTech Solutions India | 5 | 1 booked — June 20, 10am | Send prep agenda; confirm attendees by June 17 |
| DataSoft Bangalore | 3 | 0 | Send breakup email June 16; if no reply → nurture |
| CloudFirst Technologies | 7 | 1 held June 14 — proposal stage | Send proposal by June 17; follow up June 21 if no reply |

**Why these four columns and not others:** A lightweight pipeline report for a new-logo AM needs exactly this structure. "Touches sent" confirms the rep is executing; "meetings booked/pending" confirms the execution is converting. "Next action" is what separates a pipeline review from a status update — a vague entry like "follow up" gives a manager nothing to coach from, while "send proposal by June 17" immediately surfaces a risk if that date slips without movement.

Add a summary row below your account table: total touches sent, total meetings (booked + held + pending), and your pipeline velocity — the number of meetings per week at your current pace and what that implies for your close rate at the end of the month. A healthy new-logo pipeline targets 3× your monthly revenue quota in active pursuit at any given time.

## Hands-On Exercise: Your 2-Week New-Logo Sprint

**Task:** Select one net-new account and execute a fully documented pursuit sprint.

**Step 1 — Account selection gate.** Score your target account across ICP criteria and confirm the score is ≥70. Identify and date-stamp one confirmed trigger event. Name a Champion and an Economic Buyer with verified contact information. Write all three down before sending a single touch.

**Step 2 — Execute the sequence.** Send a minimum of 5 touches across email and LinkedIn over 14 calendar days. Log every touch in your CRM with date, channel, contact name, content summary, and prospect response — including "no reply" as a valid logged outcome.

**Step 3 — Evaluate performance.** Calculate your positive reply rate and compare it against the 3.43% cold email baseline and the 4–10% signal-based benchmark. Identify the one touch — by number, channel, and content type — that generated the most traction, and write one sentence naming the specific change you will test in the next sprint, with the data point that supports it.

**Step 4 — Submit your pipeline report.** Produce a four-column table (accounts contacted, touches sent, meetings booked/pending, next action) and a summary row with total touches, total meetings, and a pipeline velocity estimate.

**Success criteria:** A written three-input account selection document, a CRM log with ≥5 dated touches across ≥2 channels, a positive reply rate benchmarked against market data, one named data-supported improvement for the next sprint, and a complete four-column pipeline report ready for manager review.

The skills from ch1 through ch4 — ICP scoring, signal monitoring, sequence craft, and referral sourcing — are the inputs. What you do with them in a live, documented, evaluated sprint is how a sustainable new-logo pipeline gets built.
